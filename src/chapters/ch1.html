Chapter 1

Introduction

Where deep learning sits within AI, the three learning paradigms, and why real-world data is far less complex than its raw dimensionality suggests.

  • ScopeMachine learning fits mathematical models to observed data. Deep neural networks are currently the most powerful model class in all three paradigms.
  • Learning signalSupervised learning uses input/output pairs (x, y), unsupervised learning uses only x, and reinforcement learning uses delayed rewards.
  • Latent variablesReal data is generated by a small number of underlying factors. Generative models exploit exactly this.
§ 1 · Overview

The big picture

Artificial intelligence covers every approach to building systems that simulate intelligent behavior, including logic, search and probabilistic reasoning. Machine learning is the subset that learns to make decisions by fitting mathematical models to observed data. Deep learning is not a fourth area next to the others. It is a model class, and at the time of writing it defines the state of the art in all three learning paradigms.

The three paradigms differ mainly in what the model learns from.

§ 1.1

Supervised learning

Every real-world input, whether a sentence, an audio clip or an image, is first encoded as a vector of numbers. The model maps this input vector to an output vector, which is then translated back into a meaningful prediction. In chapter 1 the model itself is deliberately treated as a black box.

The problem type is determined by the output alone. A single continuous number makes it a regression problem, several numbers a multivariate regression problem. In classification, the model returns a vector with one probability per class.

The structure of the input

Inputs differ in more than size. Tabular data has no internal structure, so the features can be reordered and a new model trained without changing the prediction. In text, word order is part of the meaning. Images have spatial structure that is lost in the flattened vector, since two vertically adjacent pixels end up a full image row apart. Molecules, finally, are graphs of atoms and bonds.

What training means

The model is an equation that describes a whole family of possible input/output relationships. Training, or fitting, means searching this family for the member that describes the training pairs best. Because the labeled pairs act as a teacher, this is called supervised learning. Deep networks are useful because their family is extremely broad and yet easy to search. Chapter 2 makes this concrete with a straight line.

§ 1.1.5

Structured outputs

Some models do not output a single label but a whole image, a sentence or a transcription. In semantic segmentation this output structure is closely tied to the input. A pixel with a color similar to its neighbor probably has the same label. Translation, speech transcription and text-to-image synthesis are harder for two reasons. The output may be genuinely ambiguous, and it has to respect its own grammar, because not every string of words is a valid sentence and not every set of RGB values is a plausible image.

This grammar can be learned without output labels, for example from the statistics of a large text corpus. That is the link to unsupervised learning.

§ 1.2

Unsupervised learning and latent variables

Without output labels there is no supervision. The goal is then to describe or understand the structure of the data itself. The book focuses on generative models, which learn to synthesize new examples that are statistically indistinguishable from the training data. When part of the output is fixed, as in image inpainting or text completion, this is called conditional generation.

Large language models show how convincing this can be. They answer a question by generating the most likely completion of a document. According to the book, they know the statistics of language but do not understand the significance of their answers.

Almost every possible image is noise

Drawing random RGB values for every pixel practically never produces anything that looks like a photograph. Real-world images are a tiny subset of all possible images because they are generated by physical processes with few degrees of freedom. For a human face, that is roughly the 42 facial muscles.

The latent space

Latent variables z are the few underlying factors that determine a data example. By design they have a simple probability distribution, usually a standard normal. A deep network g learns the mapping from z to the data x. A new example is generated by sampling z and passing it through g.

z ∼ N(0, I)     x = g[z, ϕ]     dim(z) ≪ dim(x)

Interpolation

Since any sensible point in latent space yields a plausible example, we can interpolate between two real examples. We find their latent representations, move step by step from one to the other in latent space, and map each intermediate point back to data space. Blending pixels instead leaves the set of realistic images.

The same idea connects unsupervised and supervised learning. Instead of mapping text directly to pixels, a text-to-image model can learn a relation between the latent variables that explain the text and those that explain the image. This has three advantages. Fewer text/image pairs are needed because both sides are lower dimensional. Generated images are more likely to be plausible. And adding randomness to the mapping yields several images that all fit the same caption.

§ 1.3

Reinforcement learning

An agent lives in a world and performs actions at each time step. Actions change the state of the system, not necessarily deterministically, and may produce rewards. The goal is to learn to choose actions that lead to high rewards on average. Two problems shape the field.

Temporal credit assignmentThe reward often arrives many steps after the actions that caused it. Which of them actually mattered? In chess with a single reward for winning, the problem is extreme.
Exploration vs. exploitationKeep exploiting a known strategy with modest rewards, or explore new actions that might be better? A robot that learned to slide along on its side will never learn to walk.

Deep learning enters through the policy network. It maps the observed world state directly to an action, the board position to a move in chess, or sensor measurements to joint movements for a robot.
